CHAPTER 15

Tips and troubleshooting

Most problems have a quick fix. Start here.

No sound

  1. Open Settings › Audio and check the Output device. System default follows your computer's sound setting.
  2. Click Play test tone. You should hear a one-second beep (440 Hz).
  3. Still silent? Click Restart audio. This helps after you plug in headphones or an audio interface.
  4. Check that the track isn't muted (M) and that another track isn't soloed (S), and that the master fader is up.

Crackles, clicks or dropouts

  • Watch the DSP meter on the top display. It shows how hard the sound engine is working, and its light turns amber after a dropout.
  • Choose a bigger Buffer in Settings › Audio. Sound takes a little longer to start, but it plays more smoothly. Auto lets ChipWave choose.
  • Close other heavy programs, and bypass effects or plugins you don't need (the power button on each card, or 0 in the Rack).
  • Exports are made separately from playback, so a crackle while playing doesn't end up in your exported file.

Stuck notes

Press Panic on the toolbar, or Ctrl+.. It stops every sound at once, plugins included.

Other problems

A plugin doesn't show upCheck that it's CLAP or VST3 (not VST2), add its folder in Settings › Plugins and rescan. Look in the Browser's "skipped" list for the reason.
A song says a plugin is missingInstall that plugin on this computer. The song keeps its settings in the meantime (chapter 11).

macOS won't open the appControl-click it and choose Open, or use the Terminal command in chapter 1.
Animations are distractingTurn on Reduce motion in Settings › Look.

Good habits

  • Save often with Ctrl+S, and use Save As… for big experiments.
  • Keep the master limiter on, and leave some headroom on the faders.
  • Use the Generators to get started, then change the notes so the song becomes yours.
  • If you get stuck, every edit can be undone with Ctrl+Z.
