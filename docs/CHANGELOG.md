# ChipWave Studio changelog

Newest first. Older screenshots mentioned below live in `dist/`.

## 0.2.18.0: the Arp, an adjustable loop range, Ctrl-drag copies, Tallest Morning

Released 2026-10-27. Highlights:

- **The Arp** ([ARPEGGIATOR.md](ARPEGGIATOR.md)): a note effect that sits first in a track's Rack and plays held notes one at a time on the song grid (a Drum Kit gets it as **Note repeat**). Modes Up, Down, Up-down, Down-up, Converge, Diverge, Random (seeded), As played and Chord; 1–4 octaves walked Up, Down or Bounce; tempo-synced rates 1/4 to 1/128 with triplet and dotted values and chip fast-chord rates; gate, swing, step velocity depth, Latch and the sustain pedal. A step grid of 8, 16 or 32 steps with per-step velocity, gate, accent, rest/tie, ratchet ×1–4, transpose and seeded chance. **More ▸** holds retrigger or free-run, scale lock to the song key, humanise, Euclid fill and fast chord. Named presets; Rate, Gate, Swing, Octaves and Humanise as automation and modulation targets; **Bake Arp to notes** on the clip menu (one undo step). Live MIDI keys, note clips and tracker cells all play through it, with tracker/timeline parity (`0xy` on an Arp instrument is a chord). When the Arp is off nothing is saved and the engine plays exactly as before.
- **Fix: Length 32 shows all 32 steps.** The Arp grid always drew 16 columns, so steps 17–32 sat behind a small "1-16 ›" page switch and Length 32 looked like it did nothing. The engine and the saved song were already right; the card now widens to 32 columns (still fits 1366×768 with text at 10 pt or more).
- **Adjustable loop range** ([TIMELINE.md](TIMELINE.md#loop-region-on-the-ruler)): drag either end of the loop on the ruler to resize it (resize cursor, the end lights up), or drag the band between the ends to move it with its length kept (grab cursor). Both snap to the grid, Alt or Shift moves by single rows, the loop is never shorter than one snap step and dragging an end past the other swaps them cleanly. Double-click the band to select the clips in the loop. Undo steps **Resize loop** and **Move loop**; Shift-drag on the ruler still draws a new loop (**Set loop**). Playback and loop export use the same range.
- **Ctrl-drag copies clips:** Ctrl held when a clip drag starts makes independent copies (their own notes or pattern) that follow the mouse while the originals stay. Copy cursor and a "+ copy" badge; snaps like a move; copies go across tracks only onto tracks of a compatible kind (otherwise the drag is time-only); one undo step **Duplicate clip(s)**; a Ctrl-click without a move leaves nothing behind. Alt-drag still makes linked copies, and Ctrl+D still places a copy right after the selection. A plain move onto an incompatible track now stays on its own track.
- **New demo song "Tallest Morning":** uplifting melodic house in D major at 126 BPM, 2:17 long, in **File > Demo songs**. Two StringWave instruments carry it: echo chord stabs (chords from the Chords generator, dotted-eighth StringWave echo) and a swelling pad, pumped by the kick. It shows off the song modulation: a **Lift** macro whose route depths are automated to rise through the builds and drop back at each drop, LFOs on pad cutoff, stab pan and the lead filter, an envelope on the lead, velocity to stab brightness and random hat panning, plus filter-sweep, echo and send automation. Generated by `scripts/make_tallest_morning.py`; see [docs/research/tallest-morning.md](research/tallest-morning.md).
- **Render fingerprints:** a new `RENDER demo Tallest Morning` line was added to `render_hashes` (`0fd6da600173a315`, 3090432 frames, tracker and timeline forms equal). Every existing `RENDER` and `GEN` line is unchanged.
- Songs without an Arp save, play and render exactly as in 0.2.17.0; the song format only gains the optional Arp settings.

### Known limits
- Ctrl+D makes a linked copy (Ctrl-drag makes independent ones).
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

## 0.2.17.0: built-in user manual, digital rain About, exports end cleanly

Released 2026-10-26. Highlights:

- **Built-in user manual:** **Help > User Manual** (top of the Help menu) and a **User manual (PDF)** button on the About page open the 38-page ChipWave Studio user manual. The PDF is embedded in the app (`src/app/manual.rs`), written once to the per-user `ChipWave/Manual` folder as `ChipWave-Studio-User-Manual-<version>.pdf` and opened in the system PDF viewer, so it works on every install type and offline; if that fails, a small window offers the manual on GitHub. Source (HTML, CSS, screenshots and `build.sh`) is in [docs/manual](manual/README.md). F1 stays the octave key.
- **About opens with digital rain:** green code falls and reveals the About page as it goes, in about 1.5 s. Any key, click or scroll skips it, and Reduce motion turns it off.
- **About opens straight away:** the About window used to come out a pixel taller than itself every frame, so it grew and crept up the screen for about five seconds. The body now takes exactly what the measured footer leaves, so it opens at its final size.
- **Fix: render and export no longer replay row 0 after the song ends.** When a song reached its end, the engine wrapped round and played row 0 once more before the offline render loop noticed, so the first row rang on through the release tail of every WAV/MP3 export and offline render. Offline render and export now stop at the end (`Engine::stop_at_end`); live playback and looping are unchanged.
- **Render fingerprints changed on purpose** (accepted for this fix only). All 8 `RENDER` lines of `render_hashes` have new hashes with the same lengths; the 82 `GEN` lines are unchanged. Old and new renders were compared sample by sample: every song is bit-identical up to the frame where it ends and differs only in the tail after it (the removed replay). New reference lines:
  - Lumen Drift and the 0.2.3.5 fixture `5bcc261a9f9536b6` (was `7d4908af1125b523`), Hollow Ritual and the 0.2.3.6 fixture `4f63838ba51b44cd` (was `58134955addcf972`), Velvet Mirrorball `4996701046244965` (was `ba62f01d99da8b95`), Chrome Throat `bf8039b39cd37fbb` (was `2d1b0bd7dc762506`), Oceans 11 `9a1be90f2c8879b8` (was `19d095eb5f80e71a`), StringWave Velvet Ensemble `730775ac065c5ab5` (was `b33bd8f7c1a862f4`). Tracker and timeline forms match each other as before.
- Live playback, looping and the song format are unchanged; songs from 0.2.17.0 open in 0.2.16.0.

### Known limits
- The manual is in English only.
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

## 0.2.16.0: controller maps, named Undo, glow and shine display

Released 2026-10-26. Highlights:

- **Controller maps:** **File > Save controller map…** saves the MIDI-learned controls as a small TOML file per controller (by default in the settings folder under `Controllers`, named after the MIDI device); **File > Load controller map…** replaces the learned list with a saved one. The same two buttons sit under the learned list in Settings > MIDI. A map holds only CC numbers and knob ids (plain data, not a script); unknown ids are skipped and named in the status bar, and extra fields are ignored. See [MIDI.md](MIDI.md#controller-maps).
- **Named Undo steps:** the common edits now go through one set of named actions, so the Edit menu reads **Undo: Move clip**, **Redo: Add note** and so on, for clips (move, copy, resize, fade, delete, duplicate, split, paste), notes, tracker cells and rows, tracks and knobs. Edits not named yet still undo as plain "Undo".
- **Glow and shine display:** the top LCD has a soft backlight glow in the middle and a glassy shine across the top, tinted by the chosen backlight colour. The text keeps at least 4.5:1 contrast on the brightest spot for every backlight (new LCD tests). Paint only.
- **One knob list:** `src/knobs.rs` builds every knob of a song (instrument, patch, generator, effect, mixer, modulation, tempo) with a name and range from the existing tables. MIDI learn, controller maps, automation lane names, modulation names and generator knob names all read it, with cross-check tests so the lists can't drift apart.
- **Where does this go?** A new contributor guide, [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), says which files a new knob, effect, instrument, saved field, MIDI feature or named edit touches, and the audio-thread rules.
- Old songs save, play and render exactly as in 0.2.15.0 (checked against its renders). The song format is unchanged.

### Known limits
- Controller maps hold MIDI CC learn only (no notes, pitch bend or pressure). Loading a map replaces the learned list rather than merging it.
- Not every edit has a name yet; the rest undo as plain "Undo".
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

## 0.2.15.0: Chords that write to the timeline

Released 2026-10-25. Highlights:

- **Generators rebuilt (Chords, Melody, Bass):** the GENERATORS panel is drawn at the top level, not only inside the piano roll's chord lane, so **Write to timeline** works with no clip open, no track selected, an empty song or a drum track. It always writes notes onto a melodic track.
- **WRITE card targets:** Chords writes to **this track**, a **new track** below it, or **this clip**. A new clip goes at the loop start, else at the play marker's bar; the card says where before you write and the status bar says where after (*4 chords in a new 8-bar clip on new track 06 "Chords", song bars 33–40*). Every write is one Ctrl+Z, and clips (also trimmed or looped ones) grow to show everything written. Melody and Bass without a clip make a track at the song position.
- **Chords… on the Timeline:** a button beside **+ Add track** opens the Chords tab; with no track (or Master) selected it makes a new melodic track.
- **Chords only on pitched sounds:** one check decides whether a track's sound is percussive: drum engines and kits, plugins in the Drums category or tagged Drums/Percussion, and drum words in the sound's name (kick, snare, hat, clap, tom, perc, cymbal, rim, shaker, 808/909 kit and so on), so a synth called "Trance Kick" counts as a drum. On such a track the track pie has no Chords…, Melody… or Bass…, the piano roll has no chord lane or Generate, and the Chords… button beside + Add track is hidden. A new Chords, Melody or Bass track never copies a percussive sound.
- **Fixes:** the Write card's length no longer reads "Bars bars"; Preview plays on a melodic sound.
- Tests: 18 end-to-end generator scenarios (write targets, undo, empty song, drum tracks, hidden options, new-track sounds).
- Old songs save, play and render exactly as in 0.2.14.0 (checked against its renders).

### Known limits
- The percussive check is by engine, catalogue category and name: a pitched sound named after a drum (for example "Tom's Lead") is treated as a drum, and a drum sound with a neutral name on a plain synth is treated as pitched.
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

## 0.2.14.0: Round Robin, key pressure, clip copy and paste

Released 2026-10-25. Highlights:

- **Step conditions:** notes can play every X of Y passes, only the first time, never the first time, only in fills or never in fills, next to their Chance. New Conditions and Chance lanes in the piano roll, a **Play this note…** popover, striped notes and a live **Fill** toggle.
- **Clip Variations:** up to four takes (A–D) per note clip, one per loop pass, in order, random, random no repeat, ping-pong or every N.
- **Drum pad Round Robin:** up to four sounds per pad taking turns, with per-hit drift of pitch, level, tone, decay, timing and pan, a seed and a "Next 8 hits" preview.
- **Follow actions:** launcher clips can move on after N loops (X % Next / Again / Other / First / Last / Any / Stop), with a » badge, a "From here" preview and **Capture to timeline**.
- **MIDI key pressure (aftertouch):** poly and channel pressure from your keyboard drive a new **Pressure** modulator (quick-mod popover and Movement list), with a live meter in the MIDI light's tooltip. Recorded notes keep their pressure curves, which play back exactly; the piano roll's new **Pressure** lane shows, draws and clears them, and pressed notes get a shaded foot. See MIDI.md.
- **.mid import:** MIDI files from the browser become a note clip (notes, velocities and pressure), on the cursor track or dropped onto one.
- All of it is deterministic (seed + pass/hit), off by default and not saved when unused; old songs render bit-identically. See [ROUND_ROBIN.md](ROUND_ROBIN.md) and [MIDI.md](MIDI.md).
- **Clip copy and paste on the Timeline:** Ctrl+C / Ctrl+X / Ctrl+V (and the Edit menu) copy, cut and paste clips, snapped to the grid, with a short highlight on what was pasted. The clip pie has **Copy** and **Cut**; right-click an empty lane for **Paste here** (an independent or a linked copy; greyed "Nothing copied" while the clipboard is empty), and the track pie has **Paste at playhead**. A copy also fills the system clipboard, so Ctrl+V always arrives.
- **Drum kit fix:** step hits and starter grooves are stamped with the track's kit slot, so a kit added to a track after StringWave or WaveForge plays the kit (by every path), and a kit loaded onto a default-named track names the track.
- **Transport Loop button:** a Loop toggle (two curved arrows) beside the metronome, the same switch as **L** and the LCD's Loop pill, with the loop's bars in its tooltip.
- **Double-click a clip to loop it:** the loop is set to exactly that clip (double-click again: loop off), one undo step. Alt+double-click or Enter opens the clip. In Amiga the ruler's loop band is lifted so it shows.
- **Bigger pies:** pie menus are 30 % bigger (radii, labels, icons, titles) and trimmed to fit the window; Settings › Look › **Pie menus** sets Small, Normal or Large.
- **Amiga ⏮ fix:** the go-to-start and Loop buttons are drawn in the metronome's dimmed ink when off, so they're legible on Amiga's white buttons.
- The piano roll gives the keyboard back when you click outside it or it's hidden.
- Old songs save, play and render exactly as in 0.2.13.0 (checked against its renders).

### Known limits
- Pressure modulates per instrument, not per voice: live it follows the hardest-pressed held key of that instrument.
- .mid import ignores tempo changes, controllers and program changes, and puts every track and channel into one clip.
- Fill is a toggle, not a key you hold. Launcher clips with Variations unroll 16 passes. Tracker and live drum hit counters restart with play rather than following a seek. Timing drift only moves hits later; tone drift moves one macro per drum kind.
- Follow actions work on note clips; Capture always goes after the arrangement's end.
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

## 0.2.13.0: live recording, quick modulation and a backlit LCD

Released 2026-10-25. Highlights:
- **Live recording block:** pressing Record opens a REC block on the recording track straight away. It grows with the playhead with no length limit (it stays about two bars ahead, even past the song's end), draws each note as you play it with a short fade-in (about 200 ms), and has a red tint, a red outline, a pulsing edge and a "● REC" label. On Stop it settles into a normal clip whose length is rounded up to the bar. The whole take is one undo step; a take with no notes is dropped with a status message. Loop recording overdubs into the same block in place, and Follow keeps the playhead in view. With Motion set to reduced there is no pulse or fade.
- **Quick modulation on every knob:** a small dot on each modulatable knob (or right-click › **Modulate with…**) opens a **"Modulate <knob> with…"** popover with **LFO**, **Envelope**, **Random**, **Velocity** and **Mod wheel**. It uses the engine's own modulation where it has some (WaveForge's per-note slots, StringWave's two LFOs and mod envelope, the ChipWave synth's LFO), otherwise a song-level modulator; songs keep their format.
  - **Speed** (LFO, Random) is a labelled slider in plain words: "1/16 note", "1 beat (1/4 note)", "2 bars" up to "8 bars" with **Sync to tempo**, or 0.02 to 20 Hz free ("3.2 Hz"). Drag it, Ctrl+drag for fine steps, double-click to reset.
  - **Length** and **Attack** (Envelope) are sliders in ms / s, or beats and bars with Sync to tempo. **Smooth** (Random) glides between steps. **Amount** ("+25 %") is the only control for Velocity and Mod wheel.
  - A **live preview** draws the shape (two LFO cycles, the random steps or the envelope) with a dot moving at the real speed while the song plays.
  - Tooltips: Speed "How fast it moves", Length "How long the envelope lasts", Amount "How far it moves the knob".
  - Clicking a movement edits it with the same controls. One **Movement** list per instrument replaces the Routings card, the modulator chip row and WaveForge's Src / Dst pickers, with rows such as "LFO 1 sine · 1 beat → Cutoff · +25 %". StringWave gets a Movement strip. The engines' own routes show as rings on the knobs.
- **Tidier instrument header:** no tab counts, a Modulation dot on the tab, the "Synth" tab name, hints in a **?** tooltip, an F11 icon for full view, Save rack in a **⋯** menu, and no duplicate breadcrumb.
- **Backlit mint LCD:** the top display is now light, backlit glass with Atkinson Mono digits, toggle pills for Loop / Click / Rec / Follow, and DSP and BUF meters. **Mint** is the new default; Cyan, Amber, Green, Red and Ice are now backlight tints (saved choices keep working) and text stays at 4.5:1 contrast.
- **Keys → Notes keyboard icon:** the toolbar button that lets the computer keyboard play notes is now a keyboard icon, lit while it's on, with octave and velocity in its tooltip. It starts **on when no MIDI keyboard is connected** (system loopbacks don't count) and off when one is; once you switch it yourself, your choice is saved.
- Old songs save, play and render exactly as in 0.2.12.0 (checked against its renders); the song format is unchanged.

### Known limits
- A synced envelope **Length** is stored in seconds at the tempo it was set at, so it doesn't follow later tempo changes. StringWave's LFOs only run in Hz, so their synced speed is converted at the current tempo too.
- The ChipWave synth's own LFO syncs up to 2 bars; slower speeds use a song-level LFO when you add one.
- On WaveForge the **Envelope** uses its own filter envelope, so the popover shows no Length slider there; its Attack and Decay knobs set the length.
- The LFO shape can be changed in the popover only for song-level LFOs; engine LFOs keep the shape set on their own card.
- The preview dot runs at the real speed but isn't locked to the song position.
- The popover's source buttons can wrap "Mod wheel" onto a second row.
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

## 0.2.12.0: bigger, resizable and movable tracks

Released 2026-10-25. Highlights:
- **Taller tracks:** Timeline rows are 60 pt high by default (were 46 pt), and a tall row centres its name and buttons.
- **Resize a track:** drag the bottom edge of a track's header (the cursor changes to a resize arrow). Heights go from 28 to 200 pt, each resize is one undo step, and heights are saved with the song. **Double-click** the edge to reset the track to the default height.
- **Resize every track:** **Ctrl+wheel over the track headers**, or **Alt+wheel** anywhere on the Timeline, makes all tracks taller or shorter. The new default height is kept in the settings. Ctrl+wheel over the clips still zooms time.
- **Move tracks up and down:** drag a track's header up or down (a line shows where it lands), or use **Move up / Move down** in the track pie. The clips, instrument, colour, name and mix move with the track, and Undo puts it back.
- **Selected track stands out:** the selected track's header gets an accent-tinted fill, a wide accent bar and an accent outline, with its name in bold, and its lane gets an accent tint and outline, in every theme (in Amiga the outline is lightened so it shows on the blue lanes).
- Old songs save, play and render exactly as in 0.2.11.0 (checked against its renders). A song only stores heights for tracks you resized.

### Known limits
- In tracker mode (a song without a Timeline) track heights last for the session only and don't follow inserted or deleted tracks.
- The Song / Master row can't be resized.
- Resizing every track isn't an undo step.
- At 1366 × 768 with the lower panel open, about 3.8 tracks fit at the new default height; Ctrl+wheel over the headers makes them shorter.
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

## 0.2.11.0: easier-to-read text, UI scale and Text size

Released 2026-10-24. Highlights (details below):
- **New fonts:** every theme uses the bundled **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** for text and **Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono** for numbers, the tracker and the LCD (SIL Open Font License, credited in Help › About and `LICENSES/`).
- **One type scale:** every text size comes from one central scale, and nothing is drawn smaller than 10 pt.
- **UI scale and Text size:** Settings › Look has **UI scale** and **Text size**, each from 90 to 150 % in 5 % steps. Ctrl+= / Ctrl+- step the UI scale by 10 % and Ctrl+0 resets it. ChipWave follows the display scale you set in Windows, Wayland or X11.
- **Fits small and scaled screens:** every view fits at 1366 × 768 at 100 % and at 1920 × 1080 at 150 % in Slate, Dark and Amiga. When space is short, long names and hints end in "…" with the full text in a tooltip. The piano roll's toolbar goes to two rows with shorter button names (screen readers still get the full names), and the Rack, device chain and tracker channels scroll sideways.
- **Contrast:** Dark's secondary text is now at least 4.5:1, selected text stays readable, and text picks its colour from the surface under it.
- **Importing an instrument makes it the active one:** a .cwi from File, the Library or a drop, a WAV import, or Browser › *Add as a new instrument* now selects the new instrument. On the Timeline it goes to the selected empty track or a new track below, in one undo step.
- **One gradient over the tracks:** Timeline clips now share one gradient that runs top to bottom over the whole track area, instead of each clip having its own. Track colours and the selection highlight are unchanged.
- Old songs save, play and render exactly as in 0.2.10.0 (checked against its renders); the song format is unchanged.

### Known limits
- To fit at 1366 px, the LCD drops its GR / CLIP captions, compact drum pads keep their key and choke in the tooltip, and track names may end in "…".
- The Amiga theme's secondary text on the top background is 3.8:1; the panels meet 4.5:1.
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

### Details
- **Scaling** ([UI_SCALING.md](UI_SCALING.md)): UI scale multiplies the OS scale; Text size changes only the text. Saved zoom levels from older versions are moved into 90 to 150 %.
- **Style** ([STYLE_EMBER.md](STYLE_EMBER.md)): the type scale (10, 11, 11.5, 12.5, 13, 15, 16, 19 and 26 pt) and the contrast rules.

## 0.2.10.0: Generators (Chords, Melody, Bass), the LCD toolbar and smarter pies

Released 2026-10-23. Highlights (details below):
- **The Generators panel** has three tabs, **Chords | Melody | Bass**, with one fixed **Write** card. Drag the **⠿ GENERATORS** grip to move the panel; it stays on screen and reopens where you left it (a double-click on the grip puts it back at the top centre).
- **Melody generator:** a seeded tune over your chords, with Rhythm (Quarters, Eighths, Syncopated, Sixteenths, Long notes, Euclid), Contour, Motif, Density, Range and Octave, and Lock rhythm / Lock notes.
- **Bass generator:** a bass line that follows the chord roots in six styles: Root, Octave pump, Walking, Acid (accents and ties), Syncopated and Euclid.
- **A big Preview pad:** Preview, Dice and Another one sit in one large rounded pad beside the controls.
- **Write to timeline keeps your sound:** the new Melody or Bass track gets a copy of the track's instrument (the sound Preview played), with its settings and effects. Bass falls back to Fat Moog Bass (Acid Squelch for Acid) when the track has no instrument or plays drums.
- **LCD toolbar:** a display with tempo, position, time, signature and load, in five colours (Cyan, Amber, Green, Red, Ice) or your own, and a compact Master meter whose popover holds the Master panel. Settings › Top display turns it off for the classic toolbar, which now shows the Master meter while the Clip Arranger is hidden.
- **Context-aware pies:** the track pie's first ring follows the track (empty, drum or melodic): drum tracks get Swap kit, Drum pattern and Dice kit, melodic tracks get Instrument, Sounds, New pattern, Chords…, Melody… and Bass…. A right-click on the empty space under the tracks opens a New track pie.
- **Tracks:** Song / Master sits at the bottom of the track list, a new empty track has no instrument (an empty Rack says *Drag an instrument here*), a Drum Kit picked in the pie fills that track, and a new track with an instrument is always the active one.
- **The Rack:** a built-in instrument added from the pie or the Browser opens as its compact card, with a **Drag an effect here** box beside it (a click opens Browser › Effects). The *Choose an instrument* row is gone. A double-click on any side tab collapses or expands its card.
- **Clip Arranger:** a playing slot's ▶ shows ■ and a click stops it; the **Arranger** button (Ctrl+Shift+8) hides or shows the panel for room.
- **Piano roll:** Shift+arrows nudge the selection a semitone or a grid step; the octave move is now **Ctrl+Up/Down**.
- **Clean exit:** a normal exit leaves no recovery copy, so the recovery dialog only appears after a real crash.
- Old songs save, play and render exactly as in 0.2.9.0 (checked against its renders); the song format is unchanged.

### Known limits
- The Acid bass style has accents and ties but no slides yet, because Timeline notes can't glide.
- The Mac build is only tested on CI, and this Windows installer was built and checked on Linux.

### Details
- **Generators** ([CHORDS.md](CHORDS.md#generators-panel-chords-melody-and-bass)): open with Generate in the piano roll, the track pie's Chords…, Melody… or Bass…. Every write is one Ctrl+Z; the default target is a new track below the current one, and nothing is replaced.
- **Research credits** (ideas only, no code copied): the Melody generator draws on Computoser (Bozhidar Bozhanov), Toussaint's Euclidean rhythms (Bjorklund's algorithm), total-serialism (Timo Hoogland), isobar (Daniel Jones) and Emilie Gillet's open Eurorack firmware; the Bass generator on acid-banger (David Whiting, vitling), aciduino and JC-303 (midilab; JC-303's engine is Open303 by Robin Schmidt), acid-test (Zack Scholl) and WalkingBass (philxan). All are listed in Help › About.
- **Recovery** ([SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md)): copies put off with Later move to `recovery-archive/` (newest 10 kept) on the next clean exit; old clean-exit leftovers are removed.
- **Empty tracks** are silent until they get an instrument; a Rack drop on an empty track assigns a new instrument instead of replacing the selected one.

## 0.2.9.0: the new Rack

Released 2026-10-23. Highlights (details below):
- **The Rack.** Click a track and the lower panel shows its sound as one sideways row: the instrument first, then its effects in order, then a **+** drop zone. It replaces the Nested Device Chains tree as the main view; the tree is still there as the **chain editor** (right-click an effect › *Open in the chain editor*, **‹ Back to Rack** returns).
- **Side tabs on every card:** ⏻ bypass, ✕ remove and ‹ collapse to a slim name strip. The instrument's tab only collapses: you swap an instrument (right-click › *Replace instrument…*), you don't remove it.
- **Select a device:** click a card and it is outlined (violet for ChipWave's own instruments, the theme colour for the rest). **Delete** removes it, **Ctrl+D** duplicates it and **0** bypasses it, all undoable with Ctrl+Z, and only while the Rack was the last thing you clicked, so Delete on the Timeline still deletes clips.
- **Starter knobs:** each instrument card shows a few chosen knobs and Volume; **Advanced ›** opens the full instrument editor.
- **Drum Kit pad picker:** a Drum Kit's card shows the selected pad's knobs and a picker to choose the pad.
- **Racks in place:** a rack's layers show inside its card, each with **Open ›** to edit that layer.
- **Drag and drop:** drag a card's title to reorder; drop an effect from the Browser on the row, on the **+** zone or on a Timeline track.
- **Save rack…** next to the switch keeps the whole row as a custom rack in My Racks (also on the menus).
- **Notes | Rack:** one switch in the lower panel replaces the Piano roll, Instrument and Devices tabs (Ctrl+7 shows the Rack). With the Tracker on, Order list and Automation sit on the Notes side.
- **Browser › Instruments lists instruments only** (ChipWave's own and your plugin instruments). Presets are on the Rack card: its preset field and right-click › *Presets ›*.
- **CI and test fixes:** the Windows (MSVC) check build links again, two security advisories in the update checker's TLS crates are fixed by newer versions, and two tests that could fail on a busy machine are steady now.

It records live: the armed track (or the selected one) records, the song plays from the play marker, and every MIDI key goes in at the time you played it, with its length and velocity. Chords and overlapping notes stay as played. The notes go into the note clip under the playhead on that track, or a new **Take** clip that starts on the bar and grows with the take. With the timeline loop on, a new take starts at the loop's start, so every pass overdubs into the same clip. A take is **one undo step**. Pressing Record again stops recording and playback goes on; Stop ends the take too. The playhead runs as usual.
- **Metronome and count-in.** The toolbar's **Click** toggle (Settings › Recording › Metronome, off by default) clicks on every beat while the timeline plays. With it on, Record from stopped counts in one bar first (Settings › Recording › Count-in, on by default). Keys played in the count-in sound but aren't recorded. **Quantise on record** (off by default) snaps recorded note starts to the piano roll's grid. Exports never hear the metronome.
- **Step input is a toggle, off by default.** Before, MIDI keys on the Timeline went in one after another whenever edit mode was on and the song was stopped. That was the "records like a tracker" behaviour. Step input is now only the piano roll header's **Step input** button. The toolbar's **Rec live / Rec step** switch (the Tracker's MIDI record mode) shows only with the Tracker's tabs on, so it can't be mistaken for Record.
- **Musical typing.** **Ctrl+K**, or the **Typing** button in the toolbar or the piano roll header, turns the computer keyboard into a little MIDI keyboard. It's off by default. **A S D F G H J K L ;** are the white keys from C and **W E T Y U O P** are the black keys. **Z / X** go an octave down / up and **C / V** turn velocity down / up. The toolbar shows "Typing C4 · vel 102" while it's on. Typed notes play the selected track's instrument and go the MIDI way, so Record and Step input take them. While it's on, those letters play notes instead of their shortcuts.
- **Song / Master at the bottom.** The track list now reads: the tracks, **+ Add track**, a divider, then **Song / Master**. Its lanes follow on the timeline side and in the Clip Arranger rows, so rows stay lined up. New tracks go above it. This is display only: the song file and the sound are unchanged.

### Focus on the Timeline
- **The playhead moves again.** Pressing Play on the Timeline now moves the playhead in every song. The Timeline drew it from the engine's song position, but the engine only updated that position while it was evaluating automation lanes. Songs without automation, such as every new song, had a playhead stuck at the play marker. It is now updated on every audio callback (display only, so the sound is unchanged). Space in the Mixer, FX or Sample view plays the Timeline too, instead of looping a block that has no timeline playhead.
- **Chords in the piano roll.** Clicking several pitches at one time adds a note each time. Double-click on empty track space now makes a **note clip**, so new songs hold chords from the start. On a **Tracker clip**, which holds one note at a time per column, a chord (a second note at the same time, or the chord helper) first turns the clip into a note clip. Its linked copies switch with it, and the notes keep their instruments, volumes and effects. It is one undo step. A column with lone volume or effect commands stays a Tracker clip and says why. In timeline mode the roll never edits the Tracker's cursor block any more.
- **MIDI step input on the Timeline.** With the piano roll's **Step input** on (see Record and musical typing above: it is off by default), MIDI keys go into the piano roll's note clip at the step position. Keys held together make a chord on one step, and releasing them moves the step on by the grid. Live recording already took chords.
- **The Tracker's tabs are hidden by default.** A new setting, **Settings › Show Tracker (experimental)**, is off by default. The Tracker is still there as a specialised view: **View › Tracker**, Ctrl+1 and Ctrl+2 open it, along with its own controls and the lower panel's Order list. While the setting is off:
  - The TRACKER and ORDER tabs, View › Order list and the lower panel's Order list (outside the Tracker view) are hidden.
  - The tracker-mode toggles and Tracker-only menu items are hidden: Song › Back to tracker mode…, Add block, Block ›; View › Advanced Edit, Automation lanes, Dock lower panel under the Tracker, Alternate column shading, Row numbers.
  - The Settings rows *Alternate column shading*, *New songs* and *Opening songs* are hidden.
  - Songs open in timeline mode through the existing lossless conversion (bit-identical, markers and automation kept), so routine Timeline edits never ask to switch modes. Space and Enter play the Timeline outside the Tracker view. Settings closes back to the Timeline.
  - No tracker code or file format changed. ROADMAP: the Tracker comes back as a first-class view later.
- **One transport.** The toolbar has ⏮ · Play · Stop and the playhead position. The view bar's second set of transport buttons is gone, and so are its position and BPM readout.
- **Fewer duplicate buttons.** Removed:
  - **TIMELINE + PIANO**: the default layout. Ctrl+6 and View › Reset to default layout still give it.
  - The view bar's **PIANO · DEVICES · INST · AUTO**: they duplicated the lower panel's own tabs. Ctrl+7, Ctrl+I and the lower-panel tabs still work.
  - **View › Timeline + Piano roll**, which was the same as Reset to default layout.

### Fixes
- **The Browser can be dragged narrower again** (it bounced back wider): content wider than the panel pushed the panel back out every frame: the header's tab name and count, the preview bar's "Preview on C-3 · plays through track …" line, the Plugins tab's three buttons on one line, the Drum Patterns preview text and the Files path bar's minimum. Regression tests drag the panel narrow in every tab with long names and paths; the Inspector width and the lower panel splitter are checked the same way.
- **Saving a timeline-mode song kept its Song / Master track only until it was reopened:** loading dropped the track (and its tempo, master volume and return lanes). It now survives save and load; the self-test checks it again.
- **Windows: heavy songs no longer stutter ("331 ms · 1000+ underruns").** The Windows build used the mingw/msvcrt maths functions (`sin`, `exp`, `pow`, `tan`, `floor`…), which are 10–30x slower than on Linux and macOS, so the Chrome Throat demo needed about 130 % of a CPU core to play and the audio engine fell behind for good. Windows now uses fast, accurate maths (Rust's `libm`): Chrome Throat renders about 3x faster (roughly 45 % of real time on the test machine, close to Linux). Sound is unchanged apart from last-bit rounding on Windows; Linux and macOS output is bit-identical.
- **Faster effects everywhere:** Filter, Compressor, Delay, Drive and Reverb devices no longer recompute their coefficients (`tan`, `exp`, `pow`) every sample while a knob holds still, and a Filter with no LFO sweep skips its sine and power. The output is bit-identical (checked by a test and by rendering the demos before and after).
- **The latency readout explains itself:** the status bar shows `(max)` when the latency has grown to its 331 ms ceiling (at 48 kHz) and the tooltip and **Settings › Audio** show the **engine load** (% of real time). Above about 90 % the hint says the song is too heavy for this computer, instead of suggesting a bigger buffer, which can't help then.
- **Leaving timeline mode keeps automation exactly as it was:** a track lane that is the only lane for its parameter now goes back to the song unchanged (curves, disabled lanes and labels included) instead of being resampled row by row, so a song plays bit-identically after entering and leaving timeline mode. Parameters that also have clip lanes are still combined and sampled as before.
- **Reordering or inserting tracks no longer drops the Song / Master track** (tempo, master and return automation) in timeline mode, which changed playback.
- Tests brought up to date with intended behaviour: adding an automation lane keeps the instrument editor open; a timeline song with track lanes needs file reader version 4.

v0.2.3.16 brings **plugin instruments**: ChipWave now hosts **CLAP** instrument plugins (Surge XT and others) as normal instruments. Details: [PLUGINS.md](PLUGINS.md).

- **Scanner:** checks the standard CLAP folders for Windows, macOS and Linux, plus `CLAP_PATH` and your own folders (Settings → Plugins). Each plugin is checked in a separate ChipWave process, so crashes, exceptions and hangs (20 s timeout) can't take the app down, and Windows crash dialogs are suppressed. Results are cached and only changed files are re-checked. `chipwave --scan-plugins` prints the results. VST3 files are listed as "not hosted yet".
- **New → Plugin instrument…:** a searchable browser that scans the first time it opens.
- **Playing:** one instance per channel, created off the audio thread. The audio thread never allocates (a test checks this). Notes and velocity are sent as CLAP note events, and glide / slides as per-note tuning. Instances follow the device sample rate and block size, and play in stereo through the instrument FX chain, the mixer and the limiter. Panic reaches every instance.
- **Plugin panel:** status, **Configure…** (the plugin's own editor: embedded on Windows, floating-only elsewhere for now) and **Reload**. Eight **macros** map to any parameter. The full **parameter list** has search and live sliders, and right-click puts a parameter on a macro.
- **Macros everywhere:** `43xy` sets macro *x* from the pattern, the automation lanes offer "Macro 1–8" for plugin instruments, and MIDI learn can drive the macros.
- **State:** the plugin's preset is saved base64-encoded in `.cwt`, `.cwi` and the instrument library. It's captured before every save, autosave and export, without adding an undo step. Text songs take `patch = "clap:<id>"` with `plugin_path`, `state` and `macros`.
- **Missing plugins:** the instrument becomes a silent placeholder that keeps its data, and a warning appears when the song opens.
- **Offline render:** WAV/MP3 export and `--render` create their own instances with the saved state.
- **macOS:** `.clap` bundles load `Contents/MacOS/<binary>`.
- **Self-test:** checks the missing-plugin placeholder and the cached plugin list. `CHIPWAVE_SELFTEST_PLUGIN=<file>` also renders a note through a real plugin.
- **Tests:** ChipWave's own tiny CLAP fixture (`plugins/cw-test-synth`, also built as crash and hang copies) covers the scanner, crash/hang guard, playback, state round-trip, render, allocation-free processing, missing plugins, `43xy` and instrument renumbering. v0.2.3.11 adds a **Transform** section to the Voice instrument: three blendable stages after the formants, each with its own amount, and a master **Blend** (dry ↔ transformed; 0 = off, the default, so older songs render bit for bit as before). **Metallic resonance**: six modal resonators (constant-peak-gain two-pole form after Smith & Angell 1982) tuned to the note (± 24 st) or a fixed pitch, with harmonic, metal (TR-808), bell, bar or odd ratios, decay (ring time), damping and stereo spread; the poles always stay inside the unit circle and the output is soft-limited. **Digital distortion**: pre high-pass, drive into hard clip or an in-phase wavefold, bit depth, sample-rate reduction (raw aliasing or filtered), a ring modulator at the note × ratio and a post low-pass; every step keeps polarity, so blending never cancels. **Sub bass**: a sine one or two octaves under the note and/or an octave divider, following the voice's amplitude, saturated for growl, behind a Linkwitz-Riley crossover; below the crossover the sub is clean and the whole voice is mono (side-only high-pass, the side is ≥ 40 dB down below 120 Hz in the tests); the sub joins after the patch filter. **Formant drop** (0–12 st) lowers the formants for a monster voice. **Order**: Dist > Res or Res > Dist. Five presets: **Metal Demon**, **Digital Ghost**, **Sub Beast**, **Cyber Choir**, **Ritual Growl**. New pattern effects **`3Fxx`** blend, **`40xx`** resonance, **`41xx`** digital, **`42xx`** sub (00 = instrument, 01–FF = 0–100 %). The stages are also FX devices for any instrument (**Metal resonator**, **Digital distortion**, **Sub bass**; the resonator and the sub sine follow the channel's note; `40`–`42` set their mix), with 8 device presets. Text songs merge `[instrument.voice.transform]` tables key by key. `--self-test` renders the presets and devices through the limiter; `--screenshot --view transform`. Credits (Smith & Angell, Fletcher & Rossing, Linkwitz, TR-808, analog octave pedals) are in Help > About, the README and THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt. Details: [VOICE.md](VOICE.md#transform-02311).

v0.2.3.10 (roadmap batch 4) teaches the Voice instrument to **speak** and adds **chop / slice** to the sample editor. **Words:** a Voice has a list of up to 16 short words (`ka`, `ta`, `sha`, `dum`, …; typed in the new Words row) and each new note speaks the next one; **`3Exx`** speaks word `xx` (on a held note it says it again). Words are phoneme strings, not text-to-speech: plosives (p b t d k g) are a silent closure, a 10 ms noise burst at the place of articulation and aspiration; fricatives (s z sh f v h) are band-passed noise (z and v voiced); nasals (m n ng) are a murmur that opens into the vowel; vowels glide in from consonant **formant loci** (Delattre, Liberman & Cooper 1955; fricative and burst spectra after Stevens, *Acoustic Phonetics*). A **Speech rate** slider scales the timing (0.25–3×). **Throat mode** (Tract: Formant / Throat): instead of five formant filters, the glottal pulse drives a simulated **vocal tract**, a 44-segment Kelly–Lochbaum waveguide with a nasal branch and a tongue/lips/velum model inspired by **Pink Trombone by Neil Thapen** (MIT; reimplemented in Rust, no code copied); the vowel chips set the tongue shape, consonants close the tract at the lips, tongue blade or tip and open the velum for nasals. New presets: **Talk Ka-Ta**, **Throat Ahh**, **Throat Speech**, **Demon Words**. Throat voices cost more CPU (8 render about 6× realtime on one core). **Sample slicing** (Sample editor, new Slice row): **Transients** finds the hits (sensitivity and shortest-gap controls; slice points snap to zero crossings), **Grid** cuts into 2–64 equal parts; slices are numbered on the waveform, click one (or Prev / Next) to select it so Normalise, Reverse, Fade in/out and Gain work on that slice; Alt+click adds a slice, right-click a marker removes it. **To instruments** turns every slice into its own sample instrument (with 1 ms / 3 ms declick fades); **To notes** also writes them into the current block on the cursor channel at the rows where they fall at the song tempo, so the loop plays back and can be rearranged. One undo step. `--self-test` adds a words / throat / slicing check; `--screenshot` adds `--view speech|slice`. Credits for Pink Trombone (Neil Thapen, MIT notice included), Kelly & Lochbaum, Delattre et al. and Stevens are in Help > About, the README and THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt. Songs from 0.2.3.9 and older load unchanged (Voices without words behave as before). Details: [VOICE.md](VOICE.md), [SAMPLES.md](SAMPLES.md).
